LEGEND - what every block means
======================================================================
  [1] How much money went into or out of US spot bitcoin ETFs each day. Read the 5-day and 30-day
        totals rather than a single day, because one day is mostly noise. Negative means money is
        leaving the funds: that is demand being withdrawn, not holders selling. Grey ticks on the
        baseline are Saturdays and Sundays, when these funds do not trade. A hollow dot at the right
        end means the newest day is not published yet - normal, because a day's flow appears the next
        day, so the newest number you can have is yesterday's.
  [2] Whether US buyers are paying more or less than buyers elsewhere. When the premium is negative,
        American buyers are the missing demand. When it stays negative for weeks, it is a trend rather
        than a single quiet day.
  [3] The same premium with stablecoin noise removed, averaged over 30 days. It answers whether the US
        discount is a lasting regime or a passing distortion.
  [4] How much money is sitting in stablecoins, waiting to be deployed. When both the 7-day and 30-day
        changes are rising, new money is arriving in crypto; when both fall, money is leaving.
  [5] Whether buyers or sellers were the aggressor on each exchange. Binance (spot and perp) and
        OKX publish a daily taker split, so those three carry real 1d/7d/30d history and the net is
        ranked against every past window of the same length. Coinbase and Bybit publish only a trade
        tape with sides - no daily history - so those two stay live snapshots and never set the verdict.
  [6] How many Binance futures accounts are long versus short. Read it against the crowd, not with it:
        when almost everyone is long, a drop is more likely because there are more positions to be
        forced out. Falling long exposure among the largest traders means they are leaving. In the
        middle of the range there is nothing to act on.
  [7] What it costs to hold a leveraged position. When funding is positive, longs pay shorts, which
        tells you the crowd is positioned long. Basis is the perpetual price compared with spot.
  [8] A 0 to 100 index of crowd sentiment. Below 20 is capitulation, which has historically come
        before bounces. Above 80 is euphoria. It describes how people feel, not where price is going.
  [9] The total size of leveraged long positions on Bitfinex, as one combined book with no account
        detail. The block calculates the average price at which those positions were added. If price
        sits below that average, the recent buyers are losing money and their positions become supply
        that meets every rally. It also shows how past surges of this size resolved.
  [10] How much bitcoin was last bought at each price level. Bars above the current price are holders
        waiting to break even, which is where selling appears. Bars at or below the price show where
        support has formed before.
  [11] The current price compared with three historical reference levels. One times Balanced Price is
        where previous cycle bottoms landed. The all-time VWAP is the average price paid across the
        whole history of the market.
  [12] The average price paid by long-term holders, meaning coins last moved more than 155 days ago,
        compared with short-term holders, meaning coins moved within 155 days. Price above the long-term
        cost means patient holders are in profit and are not the sellers. Price below the short-term
        cost means recent buyers are losing money, which is where selling pressure comes from. The free
        data starts in November 2022, so this block cannot speak for earlier cycles.
  [13] Market value divided by realised value: the current price compared with the average price every
        coin last moved at. At 1.00 the market trades at break-even for the average holder. Below 1.00
        the average holder is losing money, which has only happened near cycle bottoms. The floor rises
        each cycle, so the likely bottom is a zone rather than one number. After the first reading below
        1.00, past cycles bottomed 26 to 149 days later: the crossing starts a clock, it does not mark
        the low.
  [14] A 0 to 100 momentum reading. Above 70 is overbought and below 30 is oversold. When the fast
        average sits below the slow one, the trend is still down and bounces are being sold.
  [15] A lower price low with a higher RSI at that low, which suggests sellers are running out of
        strength. Tested over nine years, a plain bullish divergence has no edge. The version that works
        needs RSI below 35 at the second low, and it won 67% of the time against a 53% baseline. A
        bearish divergence showed no edge. Only act on the oversold version, and expect roughly a 5% dip
        first.
  [16] When RSI crosses back above 40 while price is still below its 200-day average, the bounce
        usually fails at resistance. Over nine years it touched minus 3% before plus 5% in 64% of cases,
        but the average result is close to zero. Treat it as a small position with a fast exit.
